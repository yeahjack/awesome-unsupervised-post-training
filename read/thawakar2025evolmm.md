## When-to-Adapt audit

- **Auxiliary taxonomy:** `Timing Regime=Offline Corpus UPT`; `Visibility Scope=Pre-deployment corpus`.
- **Two-axis coding:** `Input Visibility=Offline`; `Update Persistence=Cumulative`; `Reset Boundary=Deployment Boundary`.

- **When the update is triggered:** updates fire during an offline RL stage before deployment; the basic unit is a group of rollouts per prompt batch.
- **Whose sample it serves:** the current rollout group's update serves subsequent training steps and the final deployed model, not the immediate inference of any single sample.
- **Whether parameters/state accumulate:** parameters accumulate across RL training; no per-sample reset.
- **Reset boundary:** it is an offline RL-style UPT schedule, not test-time arrival-by-arrival adaptation.

## 1. UPT Assignment Rationale

**Family II — Sample-Relation Supervision (trajectory-level consistency)**

EvoLMM instantiates two cooperating roles from a single backbone LMM: a **Proposer** that autonomously generates image-grounded visual questions, and a **Solver** that samples multiple answer rollouts on the same question. The training signal comes entirely from the **inter-rollout internal consistency** of the Solver's multiple answers (the degree of agreement in the empirical answer distribution), and not from any external ground truth, human annotation, or external reward model.

Specifically:
- The Solver reward is a **continuous self-consistency reward** that scales smoothly with answer agreement across multiple samples, replacing the discrete majority-vote reward.
- The Proposer reward is an **entropy-based band-pass function** of the Solver's answer entropy, encouraging the Proposer to produce questions of moderate difficulty (neither too easy nor unanswerable).
- The whole training pipeline uses only raw images, with no QA annotations, metadata, or external verifier.

This extends sample-relation internal supervision from text-only LLMs to the VLM domain; the signal source is the relational consistency among multiple trajectories generated by the model itself.

## 2. Problem Addressed

Existing LMM self-improvement methods rely on either (i) human-annotated data or metadata, or (ii) an externally defined reward model or evaluator (e.g. knowledge-distilled reward networks). These dependencies limit autonomy and scalability.

Moreover, in multimodal settings, naively applying a discrete majority-vote reward (as in SQLM) works poorly — the Solver's outputs are highly unstable early in training, leading to many zero-reward updates and unstable optimization.

EvoLMM aims to enable LMM reasoning to self-improve under **fully unsupervised** conditions (using only unlabeled images), driven by internal-consistency signals.

## 3. Method

### Overall framework

Starting from a single pretrained LMM (e.g. Qwen2.5-VL-7B), instantiate two lightweight roles via LoRA adapters:

- **Proposer** $\pi_\phi(q|x)$: given an unlabeled image $x$, generate a visually grounded question $q$.
- **Solver** $\pi_\theta(y|x,q)$: sample $N$ independent answers $y_{1:N}$ for question $q$ and compute the empirical answer distribution $p(a|x,q)$.

### Continuous self-consistency reward (Solver side)

For each sampled answer $y_i$, the reward is defined as:

$$r_i^{\text{sol}} = \big(p(y_i | x, q)\big)^\gamma \cdot \Big(1 - \lambda_{\text{len}} \cdot \max\{0,\, (w_i - \tau)/\tau\}\Big)$$

where $p(y_i|x,q)$ is the agreement score of this answer in the empirical answer distribution, $\gamma \in (0,1]$ controls reward softness (smaller values amplify intermediate probability differences), $w_i$ is the number of tokens before the `<answer>` tag in the response, and $\tau$ is the target conciseness threshold. This continuous reward scales smoothly with answer consistency, producing meaningful positive gradients even with partial agreement (e.g. 2/5 consistent), avoiding the sparsity and instability of discrete rewards.

### Entropy-based continuous Proposer reward

Compute the consensus entropy $H(x,q)$ of the Solver's answer distribution; the Proposer reward is a Gaussian band-pass function:

$$r^{\text{prop}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{(H(x,q) - \mu_H)^2}{2\sigma_H^2}\right)$$

The Proposer receives the maximum reward when the Solver's uncertainty is in a medium range (neither over-confident/trivial nor too uncertain/unsolvable). This drives the Proposer to ask near the Solver's decision boundary, forming an **adaptive curriculum**: as the Solver improves, the Proposer must produce harder questions to stay within the entropy band.

### Training and optimization

Both roles are optimized with REINFORCE policy gradient, plus:
- **Exponential moving-average baseline** $b_A$ for variance reduction.
- **Token-level KL regularization** (against a frozen reference model), adaptively controlled by a dynamic KL controller adjusting $\beta_A$.
- The Solver updates every step; the Proposer updates every $K=5$ steps.

## 4. Datasets

### Training data
Uses **raw images only** (no QA pairs, no metadata). About 1,000 images sampled from each of 6 datasets, totaling ~6k images:
- ChartQA, AI2D, InfographicVQA, PlotQA, ChartX, Geometry3K.
- Covers charts, scientific diagrams, geometric figures, and other diverse visual content.

### Evaluation data
Eight multimodal math and visual-reasoning benchmarks (using each official evaluation split):
- **ChartQA**, **MathVista**, **MathVision**, **MathVerse**, **InfoGraphic-VQA**$_{\text{val}}$, **AI2D**, **ScienceQA**, **MMMU**$_{\text{val}}$.

## 5. Evaluation metrics and main results

### Metrics
All benchmarks use their standard accuracy protocols, with unified inference via the lmms-eval framework.

### Main results (Table 1, Qwen2.5-VL-7B)

| Benchmark | Baseline | EvoLMM (Ours) | $\Delta$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChartQA | 84.00 | **86.70** | +2.7% |
| MathVista | 68.46 | **70.52** | +2.06% |
| MathVision | 23.91 | **24.81** | +0.9% |
| MathVerse | 43.78 | **44.88** | +1.1% |
| InfoGraphic-VQA | 80.44 | **81.06** | +0.62% |
| AI2D | 82.61 | **83.41** | +0.8% |
| ScienceQA | 88.30 | **89.50** | +1.2% |
| MMMU | 51.11 | **52.01** | +0.9% |

### Key findings

1. **Continuous reward substantially beats discrete reward:** discrete majority-vote adaptation yields only +0.3–0.6% (sometimes degrading); the continuous self-consistency reward steadily improves all benchmarks by +2–3%.
2. **Cross-backbone generalization** (Table 3): the same framework applied to InternVL3-8B, Gemma3-12B-It, and Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct yields consistent +2–3% gains — architecture-agnostic.
3. **Model-scale scaling** (Table 4): 72B models gain even more in absolute terms (ChartQA 88.20→91.04; MathVista 73.93→76.44).
4. **LoRA is optimal** (Table 2): LoRA outperforms QLoRA and full fine-tuning in the self-evolution setting; full fine-tuning overfits and degrades without external supervision.
5. **Emergent curriculum:** during training the Proposer automatically shifts from easy to moderately hard questions, forming an implicit curriculum.
