## 1. UPT Assignment Rationale
SePT is a strict UPT candidate. It proposes reward-free online self-training: from the current policy, the model self-samples reasoning traces on answer-less prompts and trains directly on those self-generated traces with the standard SFT / NLL objective; the updated model then continues to generate the next round of training data. The whole process uses no rewards, verifiers, or teacher-provided solution traces.

Under the current taxonomy, it belongs to **Family III: Self-Generated Target Bootstrapping**. The training target is the model's own sampled trajectories—not an entropy / gradient / statistic reward, and not sample-relation consensus. Compared with vanilla self-training, SePT's emphasis is online refresh: every round resamples from the updated model, avoiding the weak gain from a fixed offline self-generated set.

## 3. Method

SePT's algorithm is simple:

1. Sample prompts from the training question set.
2. With the current model, generate `G` outputs at sampling temperature `tau_s` (default `G=1`).
3. Add `(q, o)` to the current self-training batch.
4. Minimize a standard NLL / SFT loss at training temperature `tau_t`.
5. Use the updated model for the next round of self-generation.

The paper interprets one SePT round as a forward-KL projection: the student model is pulled toward the current low-temperature self-teacher distribution. The key empirical setting is `tau_s < tau_t`; low-temperature sampling preserves local token ordering and amplifies the margin. Unlike RLVR / GRPO, SePT has no reward-weighted policy optimization and no external verifier.

## 4. Datasets

The main training question set is DeepScaleR (DSR); a second training question set is OpenThoughts-Math (OTM). The paper emphasizes using questions / prompts, not using answers as supervision.

Evaluation covers six math-reasoning benchmarks:

- MATH-500
- AMC-23
- Minerva Math
- OlympiadBench
- AIME-24
- AIME-25

The authors also do a contamination audit between DSR / OTM and the evaluation sets. DSR contamination is clearly higher than OTM's, especially against MATH-500 and OlympiadBench; OTM is less contaminated. When listing this work in the survey, this should be flagged as a prompt-source caveat.

## 5. Evaluation metrics and main results
On Qwen2.5-Math-7B, SePT raises six-math-benchmark average Pass@1/8/32 from 22.7 / 47.3 / 61.0 to 39.5 / 57.7 / 67.9, and AVG from 43.7 to 55.0. On Qwen2.5-7B, AVG climbs from 44.1 to 50.7. Improvements on instruction-tuned models are smaller; the paper explicitly states that SePT's effectiveness depends on the model family and initial distribution.

Compared with GRPO, SePT typically lags but uses far less compute; the gap to GRPO shrinks on the OTM question set. Compared with EM-FT, SePT is stronger because it trains the entire self-generated trajectory, rather than doing token-level entropy minimization only on visited prefixes.

The offline version SePT (Offline) gains markedly less than the online-refresh version, indicating that the update-bearing self-generated-data loop is core to the method.

## 6. Position in the UPT Survey
Recommended as a new Family III candidate. It is a clean reward-free self-generated-target baseline that contrasts cleanly with Self-Tuning, Self-Improve, G-Zero, and similar methods: no verifier, no reward model, no preference optimization—just online SFT on self-generated trajectories.

Recommended short label: `SePT`.
Recommended family: `Self-Generated Target Bootstrapping`.
Main-table caveat: training question sets come from DSR / OTM, and DSR has contamination risk; but the training signal itself uses no answers, verifiers, or teacher traces.
